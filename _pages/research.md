My research connects protein dynamics to cellular function through theory, computation, and experiments. I am interested in both the physical mechanisms that govern biological systems and the statistical tools that let us infer those mechanisms from data.

Protein dynamics and regulation
======

I investigate how conformational dynamics govern protein recognition, folding, and function. My work on GroEL/S and dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) connects molecular motions to chaperone engagement and antibiotic resistance. This work examines how chaperones can reshape the behavior of folded proteins and influence small-molecule binding.

I also study how the surrounding water network influences protein conformations. Combining molecular simulation, experiments, and statistical mechanics, I investigated the entropy-driven mechanism of salt-induced protein conformational changes.

Related work: [chaperonin recognition and drug resistance](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.03.729952) · [entropy-driven protein denaturation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61959-9).

Cellular organization and multiscale models
======

I study how interactions among biomolecules produce collective behavior. My work on enzymatic metabolons uses coarse-grained simulations and reaction–diffusion models to understand how enzyme clustering accelerates biochemical reactions. Related collaborative work examines how complete enzyme assemblies support substrate channeling in coenzyme Q biosynthesis.

I am also interested in the physical principles of biomolecular condensates, including how polymer conformations and binding entropy affect their organization and material states. These questions motivate my broader interest in connecting molecular models to predictive descriptions of cellular function.

Related work: [enzymatic metabolons](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37915170/) · [coenzyme Q biosynthesis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74806-2) · [sticker-spacer condensates](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.09.758027) · [condensate states](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36350032/).

Statistical inference and AI for science
======

I am interested in combining statistical inference, physical models, and machine learning to extract mechanisms from biological data and guide protein design. My collaborative work on VibeProteinBench evaluates language-based protein-design systems.

Looking ahead, I want to connect these approaches with experiments and models of cells: learning from measurements, making testable predictions, and improving our understanding of how molecular perturbations affect cellular behavior.

Related work: [VibeProteinBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10978).

Long-term direction
======

My goal is to develop predictive models across biological scales, from proteins to cells, and use the combination of theory, computation, and experiments to help understand and treat disease.
