I am a biophysicist interested in how protein dynamics and interactions shape cellular function. My research combines **theory, computation, and experiments**, with interests in **protein dynamics, statistical inference, and AI for science**.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemistry at Harvard University in August 2026, advised by Professor Eugene I. Shakhnovich. My doctoral research connected protein conformational dynamics to chaperone recognition, protein folding, enzyme organization, and biomolecular condensates. Before Harvard, I received my B.S. in Chemistry from Fudan University.

My long-term goal is to build predictive models that connect molecular mechanisms to cellular behavior, and to use those models alongside experiments to help understand and treat disease.

Research interests
======

* **Protein dynamics and regulation:** how molecular motions govern protein recognition, folding, and function.
* **Cellular organization and modeling:** how interactions among proteins give rise to collective behavior, metabolism, and cellular function.
* **Statistical inference and AI for science:** combining physical models, biological data, and machine learning to uncover mechanisms and guide experiments.

Read more about my [research](/research/) and [selected publications](/publications/).

Teaching and community
======

At Harvard, I taught courses in statistical mechanics, thermodynamics, and chemistry, and mentored graduate and undergraduate researchers. I also organized the Greater Boston Area Theoretical Chemistry Seminar Series in 2023–2024. See [teaching and mentorship](/teaching/) and my [background](/cv/) for details.
